## Context

SafeBulk is a bulk product editor for Shopify merchants who need to change many products at once. I built it with a backend engineer: two people, no product manager and no designer. I had three reasons to start it: to own a Shopify product **end to end**, not just features inside someone else's app; to have something real and public to show for my Shopify experience; and to find out how far an **AI-first** workflow could take a whole product, from the market question to the App Store.

## Problem

- **Where is there room?** Large players already cover much of the Shopify App Store. We needed a niche they had not locked down.
- **Bulk edits are risky.** One wrong rule can change hundreds of products before anyone notices, and edits run as long jobs in the background.
- **Two people, one product.** With no PM or designer, every requirement, screen and API contract had to be decided by us — clearly enough that frontend and backend could build in parallel.

## Solution

**Find the gap first.** We used Gemini and Claude to map Shopify niches that the big apps had not taken over. Of the six ideas they surfaced, bulk editing stood out: few large players, and the existing competitors had clear weak spots. Their 1–3 star reviews (gathered with AI assistance) kept repeating three complaints, and each one became a feature:

| What merchants complained about | What SafeBulk does |
| --- | --- |
| **Undo that failed halfway**, leaving the catalog half old, half new | A **history log** of every run with its status, and an **undo** for each one |
| **No preview** — configure rules, apply blindly, hope for the best | A **preview of every change before it is applied**, each row flagged by severity |
| **Slow, confusing UI** — huge lists and a wall of dropdowns | A guided **3-step Bulk Edit Wizard**: filter → configure rules → preview |

**Write it down before building it.** I wrote a detailed business-logic spec for every feature and aligned the API contract with the backend before either of us wrote code, so we could build in parallel without rework. I sketched the screens in **Figma AI** to settle layouts and flows quickly.

**Build AI-first, with guardrails.** I built the entire front end with **Claude**: we planned features together, I wrote **project rules** the AI had to follow, and every feature started from its spec as the input. The MVP shipped in **about 1.5 months**:

- **Bulk Edit Wizard** — filter products, configure rules, preview with per-row severity, then apply.
- **CSV import/export** — with a validation preview so errors surface before an import starts.
- **History log** — every run with its status, and undo.
- **Plan gating** — paid tiers and quotas, including the case where a quota limit is hit while a job is running.

Long-running jobs are tracked with React Query conditional polling, and their state lives in the URL, so a job survives a refresh and can be reopened from a link.

**Ship it.** I containerized the front-end build stage with a Dockerfile. The app is live on the Shopify App Store.

## Takeaways

- **Order matters more than tools.** If I started again I would follow the same sequence: market and niche → product idea and feature list → a spec per feature → UI mockups → rules for the AI → separate FE and BE specs for each feature. Each step made the next one faster.
- **AI speeds up the work you have already defined.** Clear specs and project rules are what made an AI-first workflow fast instead of chaotic.
- **Competitors' bad reviews are a roadmap.** The three most repeated complaints decided the three most important features.
- **Owning the product changes the questions you ask.** At work, many architecture and library choices were made for me. Here every choice was mine — and so was the market question behind it.
